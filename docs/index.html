Hypothesis 1

Respondents who spent most of their childhood in France rate infidelity as less morally wrong than respondents raised elsewhere.

Variables and descriptive statistics

  • Country raised (raisedFrance): A categorical variable with two groups: France and Elsewhere. It was recoded from the free-text country where the respondent spent most of their childhood.
  • Moral wrongness of sexual infidelity (infmoral_sex): Rating from 1 (Not at all) to 7 (Extremely). Higher values indicate that sexual infidelity is judged as more morally wrong.
  • Moral wrongness of emotional infidelity (infmoral_emo): Rating from 1 (Not at all) to 7 (Extremely). Higher values indicate that emotional infidelity is judged as more morally wrong.

Observed trend

Respondents raised in France rated sexual infidelity as less morally wrong on average (M = 5.33, median = 6) than respondents raised elsewhere (M = 6.00, median = 7), although the distributions overlap substantially, and both groups used the full 1 to 7 range. The same direction appeared for emotional infidelity (M = 4.49 for France vs. 5.42 for Elsewhere). The France group (n = 2,074) was much smaller than the Elsewhere group (n = 84,092), so its distribution should be interpreted cautiously. These are descriptive patterns, not a test of the hypothesis.

H2. Appearance time and jealousy

Respondents who spend more time per day on their appearance are more jealous about their partner’s sexual infidelity.

Variables and descriptive statistics

  • Daily time spent on appearance (appearTime): The sum, in minutes, of eight items about daily time spent on makeup, hygiene, cosmetics, exercise, hair, dressing, diet, and other appearance activities. Each item ranges from 0 to 360 minutes (360 means 6 hours or more). The total is missing if any of the eight items is missing.
  • Jealousy about a partner’s sexual infidelity (jealsex): Rating from 1 (Not at all) to 7 (Extremely). Higher values indicate more jealousy.

Observed trend

Code

round(r, 2)
[1] -0.26

The correlation was -0.26, indicating a weak negative association. Respondents who reported spending more time on their appearance tended to report less jealousy about their partner’s sexual infidelity, which is the opposite of the direction H2 predicted. This is a descriptive pattern, not a test of the hypothesis.
